\begin{abstract}
% (1) Problem
Many Vietnamese folk board games survive only by word of mouth and are
easily forgotten. \textbf{Cờ Trận} is one such
game. It was played in Đồng Bùa hamlet, Tam Quan commune, Tam Đảo district,
Vĩnh Phúc province, using whatever could be picked up by the roadside as
pieces: stones, pebbles, twigs, leaves. To our knowledge it has never been
written down.
% (2) What we did
This paper records the rules of Cờ Trận for two, three and four
players, from the recollection of the author, who played it throughout
grades 5 to 9. The English name \emph{Tactical Formation} was given by the author. A
piece moves by jumping over an adjacent piece of any side, and the
jumped-over piece stays on the board. After landing, if a line of enemy pieces lies
between the moved piece and a friendly piece, the player chooses one such
line and that whole line changes side. The board has two layers of positions,
vertices and centres: a vertex piece moves in 8 directions and a centre
piece in 4.
% (3) Results
We give a formal mathematical model of the two-player mode, in which the
total number of pieces is an invariant: capture is a change of side, never
a removal. We also implement the game as open-source software, with an AI
based on Minimax search with Alpha-Beta pruning (the Paranoid variant for the
multiplayer modes). Experiments measure the branching factor, game length
and the bots' think time. In the two-player mode deeper search wins
consistently, and in 100 games between two bots of equal depth the loser was
always blockaded with pieces left; no game ended by losing all pieces.
% (4) Significance
Through a written record, a formal model and software that anyone can play,
the paper helps preserve a folk game that is fading away.
\end{abstract}
